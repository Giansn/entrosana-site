The short version. We collect as little as the service needs. No advertising, no analytics, no tracking, no third-party content on our pages. Most of what you do in the Hub stays in your browser. What you send us is used to answer you, to review what you propose, and, if you ask for it, to publish under your name. Everything has a deletion date, and you can delete your own data yourself.

Where your data rests, and where it is processed. Our residency promise covers storage: your data rests on our own server in Switzerland, with one encrypted backup copy at Cloudflare R2. Processing is a separate matter. Delivery runs through Cloudflare (USA), our code lives at GitHub (USA), and mail to sygnif@entrosana.com is read by Claude, a language model run by Anthropic (USA). Where our pages say «Swiss/EU», they mean the residency of stored data, not the seat of every provider that processes it.

1. Visiting our pages

Both sites are delivered through Cloudflare, Inc. (San Francisco, USA), which terminates the encrypted connection at its edge and forwards requests to our own server in Switzerland. Cloudflare sees your IP address, browser identification and the request; it may set a short-lived security cookie only when it challenges a request it considers suspicious. We set no cookies ourselves. Cloudflare’s security features may inject a small script into pages; it does not track you.

Our origin server keeps a request log: IP address, path, status, duration and, if you are signed in to the Hub, your account number. The Hub’s log is deleted after 14 days. The website’s log has no fixed deletion period yet; we say so here rather than promise one.

Fonts and scripts come from our own domain. Loading a page contacts nothing else.

2. What stays in your browser

The Hub keeps your projects, their steps, your record trail, your theme choice and, after you sign in, your sign-in token in your browser’s local storage. None of it is sent to us unless you submit a proposal, a request or an application. Clearing your browser’s site data removes it.

3. Your account

You can register with an e-mail address; we send a link, and opening it signs that device in. We store your address, the time you registered, a hashed token per signed-in device (valid 30 days), and what you add to your profile: your name, organisation or school, role, a line about yourself, a website, languages. Everything except the address is optional; a name is needed before you can contribute, because contributions are published under it.

Purpose: to know whom we are talking to, to publish under your name, to decide on applications and proposals. Unconfirmed addresses are deleted after 7 days. Sign-in links expire after 20 minutes, confirmation links for proposals after one hour. When a device signs in to an existing account, the account’s address gets a mail with time and coarse origin; under «Devices» on /me every session can be ended, singly or all at once. Erasing your data or changing the attribution of a module needs a sign-in link younger than 15 minutes. Accounts are for people of 13 and older; younger learners take part through a teacher or a parent, who holds the account. Under /me you can change your profile, download everything we hold (JSON), sign out of a device, and erase your account. Erasing removes address, profile and applications; published modules remain only where you chose that they should. An account with no sign-in for 24 months gets a notice by mail; if nobody signs in within the three months after it, the account is erased the same way, and published modules remain under your name only where you chose that they should.

Everyone who registers is a collector. Contributor standing is granted by our team after an application (a text you write, kept with your account; decided applications are deleted after 24 months).

Your public profile. Every account with a name has a public page under /p/<number>: name, standing, picture (if you add one), organisation or school, role, the line about you, website, languages, the date you joined, your published modules, and the journal entries you marked public. Nothing else, and never your address. You choose per journal entry whether it is public; you can change or delete entries, remove the picture, and empty the profile fields at any time under /me. The picture is stored as a small image (256 × 256) with your account and deleted with it.

Search. The search on our pages runs in your browser for pages and the catalogue and asks our server only for people, public journal entries and published modules. We do not log what you type; the server records the request path, not the query.

4. Proposals and published modules

A proposal contains your name, address, title, area, the text, links to files you host elsewhere and, if you attach it, the part of your record trail that belongs to the project (step texts and hashes; other projects appear as hashes only). We record each step (received, confirmed, in review, decided, published) with the time and the team member who took it, and we mail you the decision with its reason.

A proposal that is never confirmed is deleted after 7 days. A confirmed proposal stays until it is decided and, if published, for as long as the module is in the catalogue. Published modules carry your name under AGPL-3.0; the catalogue file lives in our code repository at GitHub, Inc. (USA). You can withdraw or delete an undecided proposal yourself, and choose whether a published module keeps your name if you erase your account.

5. Requests

If you ask us for a module, a track, a tool or a service under /request, we store name, address, organisation, kind and text for 180 days, send the text to info@entrosana.com and you a short confirmation without your text. No account is needed.

6. Mail and phone

Our mailboxes are hosted at Proton AG, Geneva, Switzerland. Confirmation, decision and notification mails are sent from our account through Proton’s servers. Mail to sygnif@entrosana.com is read by our own software on our server in Switzerland, which passes the content to Claude, a language model run by Anthropic, PBC, San Francisco, USA, to sort it and to draft replies; people decide what goes out. Mail content therefore reaches a provider in the USA. Anthropic works under standard contractual clauses; its privacy policy of 8 July 2026 names no Data Privacy Framework certification. What you tell us by phone is noted only as far as needed to answer you.

7. Our own record

The Hub keeps a chain of hashed entries about every step it takes: who (as an account number) did what and when. It contains no names, addresses or texts, only a truncated hash of an address where a confirmation was requested. It is kept permanently because it proves that the history of a module was not rewritten. Rate limits (five actions per hour per address and per IP) are counted in memory for one hour and not stored.

8. Backups

Encrypted backups of the database, the site and the Hub are written nightly: to our server, to a second disk at the same place, to our own Nextcloud on the same server, and one encrypted copy to Cloudflare R2 (Cloudflare, Inc.). Encryption happens before anything leaves our server; the providers cannot read the copies. Backups are kept without a fixed limit for the time being; a retention period has not been set yet. Once it is, this statement is updated.

9. Who else receives data

Cloudflare, Inc., USA (delivery, security, tunnel, one encrypted backup copy); Proton AG, Switzerland (mail); GitHub, Inc., USA (code repository, which includes the catalogue with published contributors’ names); Anthropic, PBC, USA (the language model that reads the sygnif@ mailbox). Cloudflare and GitHub state in their privacy statements that they are certified under the Swiss-U.S. Data Privacy Framework (checked 9 September 2026); Anthropic relies on standard contractual clauses, as does everything else that the framework does not cover. We sell nothing and share nothing for advertising. A teacher or a school never receives more than what you publish.

10. Your rights

You may ask what we hold about you, have it corrected or deleted, receive it in a portable form, and object to its processing (Art. 25, 28 and 32 DSG; Art. 15 to 21 GDPR). Under /me and /contribute/status you can do most of this yourself. For the rest, write to info@entrosana.com or to the address above; we answer within 30 days. You may complain to the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (FDPIC), Feldeggweg 1, 3003 Bern, or, in the EU, to your supervisory authority. We make no automated decisions about you.

11. Children

Our modules are used in schools. We do not knowingly collect data of children, and we ask teachers never to send us the personal data of pupils; a module report needs the group, not the names. If you believe we hold a child’s data, write to us and we delete it.

Schools: the Hub holds no pupil accounts and no pupil data. Teachers and schools register as adults; a module report names the group, never the child. Our processing register (the section at the end of this statement) is available on request at info@entrosana.com.

12. Changes

We change this statement when the service changes and note the date at the top.
